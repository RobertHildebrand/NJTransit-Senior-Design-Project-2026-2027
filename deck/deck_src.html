BackgroundThe line

A 34-mile diesel light rail line, mostly on one track

  • Opened March 2004 on a former freight line. Freight trains still use it at night, so passenger service runs about 6 am–10 pm.
  • Seven single-track sections. Opposing trains can only pass on double track, so the timetable is built around those meets.
  • 72 grade crossings, 23 bridges. Trains are at most 2 cars long.

BackgroundWhat we are dealing with

Camden to Trenton along the Delaware, through ten river towns

  • Connects: NJ TRANSIT Northeast Corridor, Amtrak and SEPTA at Trenton; the Atlantic City Line at Pennsauken; PATCO at Walter Rand in Camden.
  • Shares the track with freight. Freight trains run overnight, so passenger service must fit between about 6 am and 10 pm.
  • Runs through town centers: street running in Camden and Trenton, 72 grade crossings, and platforms that take one train at a time on single-track sections.
  • Small, aging fleet: 20 diesel cars built in 2002–03. Today's timetable needs 8 in service at the peaks, before spares and rescues.

BackgroundWhat makes it hard to run

Street running, freight neighbors and meets on one track

72 grade crossings and street segments add variability to running times, which is what makes trains late in our simulation.

Temporal separation: passenger service ends before freight takes over at night, so late trips can't simply be pushed later.

Seven single-track sections: a late train at one meet holds the opposing train, which is then late for its next meet.

BackgroundThe client's question

“We have N working cars at 6 am. What should we run today?”

One trackEvery train must meet opposing trains on double track. Adding or removing a train changes every meet.
Two cars per failureA breakdown ties up a second car to rescue it, and on single track it blocks both directions.
No plan BThere is one published weekday timetable. When cars are short, trips are cancelled one by one.

The decision happens every morning. It depends on how many cars pass inspection, which depends on how reliable the fleet is and how fast repairs come back.

BackgroundProblem statement

Problem statement

River LINE riders do not get the service the timetable promises. In 2024–25 only 80–87% of trips arrived on time, against 97–99% on NJ TRANSIT's other light rail lines. On days when too few cars pass inspection, trips are cancelled one at a time, without notice. Our model puts the combined loss at about 31% of scheduled trip-miles in 2024–25 and 16% in 2026.

1 · Late trains spreadOn single track, one late train holds every opposing train due to meet it. Today's timetable schedules 36 meets a day with less than a minute to spare.
2 · One plan for every dayThe single published timetable needs 8 trains. With fewer cars, the shortfall shows up as unplanned cancellations and longer gaps that riders learn about at the platform.
3 · An unreliable fleetMiles between failures fell from about 22,000 (2022) to 2,377 (Sept 2025). Each failure takes a second car to rescue it and blocks the single track.

Measured by reliable trip-miles delivered: trip-miles that run and arrive less than 6 minutes late, as a % of today's timetable. Proposed target: 90%.

BackgroundObjectives · requirements · scope

What success looks like

Objectives (measurable; targets to agree with NJ TRANSIT)

  1. Reliable trip-miles delivered ≥ 90% (model: 84% in 2026 conditions)
  2. On time ≥ 95% of trips run (NJ TRANSIT's goal is 97%)
  3. Unplanned cancellations ≤ 1% of scheduled trips
  4. No longer gaps than today (30 min at the peaks) and no more than today's 109 train-hours a day

Client requirements

  • Choose the day's schedule from the 6 am hold sheet (cars available)
  • Weigh on-time performance, frequency and rider impact
  • Every meet on double track; freight window kept
  • A tool a dispatcher can use; open-source solvers only
  • Built on public data now, re-run on NJ TRANSIT data later

Scope

In: weekday timetables, the daily plan by car count, delay and fleet simulation, fleet reliability requirements.

Out: weekends and events, crew rostering, bus bridges, fares, and design of new track.

Reliable trip-miles delivered = trip-miles that run and arrive less than 6 minutes late, as a % of today's timetable. It counts a cancelled trip and a late trip as lost service in one number.

Background2022–2026

What today's timetable gets right

Its 15/15/30-minute peak spacing fits 8 trains into a 150-minute round trip. Even 20-minute spacing would need a 9th train. The 30-minute gap also hides recovery time.

What it lacks

No smaller timetables ready for short days, no rule for when to hold a spare car, and no way to tell riders the day's plan before they leave home.

FindingTimetable

Evening out the gaps alone makes trains later

30 → 21longest morning gap (min), today → evenly spaced T8
82% → 69%trips on time, today → T8 (2024–25 conditions)
7% → 22%meets with less than 1 minute of slack
9 → 6median layover at the ends (min)

Packing the trains evenly uses up the slack. A train one minute late then holds the opposing train at the siding, and the delay spreads down the line.

The fix is slack in the right places: at least 2.5 minutes at every meet and 9-minute layovers. Moving slack into holds before single track, longer layovers alone, or bigger meet buffers alone each recover only part of the loss.

ReferenceKey terms

Key terms

Single track
A stretch with one track; only one direction can use it at a time.
Meet
Where two opposing trains pass. Must happen on double track.
Spare time at a meet
Minutes between one train leaving the single track and the opposing train being due to enter it.
Layover
Time a train waits at the end of the line before its next trip.
Hold sheet
The 6 am list of cars fit for service that day.
On time
Arrives less than 6 minutes late (NJ TRANSIT counts 0:00–5:59).
MDBF
Mean distance between failures, in car-miles.
Knock-on delay
Minutes a train waits at a siding for a late opposing train.
Reliable trip-miles
Trip-miles that run and arrive on time, as a % of today's timetable. Our headline measure.
B8, B7, T8
Timetables we designed: B = buffered meets, T = evenly spaced; the digit is trains at the peak.
