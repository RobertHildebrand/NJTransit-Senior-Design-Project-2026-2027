Current and proposed timetables

NJ TRANSIT's published weekday timetable (GTFS, Sept 2026), six proposed pattern timetables (T8–T3) and five uniform-headway timetables (U8–U4). Pattern timetables keep NJT's 150-minute round trip, space trains as evenly as the single track allows, and have every meet on double track all day. Uniform timetables are conflict-free within each period but need retiming where peak and off-peak meet (see the conflicts column).

Proposed schedules: what each one buys, and what it costs

Each proposal changes one thing about the timetable. Scheduled quality (trains, train-hours, gaps, average wait) comes from the timetable itself. Punctuality comes from a delay simulation: 400 simulated weekdays with random running times and incidents, calibrated so NJ TRANSIT's current timetable is 83% on time (it reported 80–84% in 2023–25). A late train that is due to meet an opposing train on single track delays that train too. “Hot day” adds 6% to running times, like a heat speed restriction. Click a row to replay it below.

About this model and its data

  • Track and stations: real track geometry from NJ TRANSIT's GTFS shape; single and double track from the NJDOT Freight Guideway fact sheet (2021), rescaled from railroad mileposts.
  • Tiered timetables: for each headway, a periodic single-track timetable MIP (HiGHS) places every opposing meet on double track and returns the trains needed: 15 min → 11, 20 min → 8, 30 min → 6.
  • NJT published: the actual Sept 2026 weekday timetable (87 trips, 12 vehicle blocks). A block with no car available is cancelled.
  • Breakdowns: Poisson in car-miles, scaled by season. Calibrated scenarios (report Section 9) use NJ TRANSIT's monthly River LINE series: 3,661 miles between failures in 2024–25 (S1) and 3,568 in Mar–Aug 2026 (S2, the default). A failure needs a rescue car: a protect car from where it is staged if one is free, otherwise the nearest train in service is pulled. A failure on single track blocks both directions.
  • Rider waiting: random arrivals over each gap between departures, weighted by an assumed hourly profile on about 5,600 weekday boardings.
  • Costs: $547 per train-hour = NTD FY2024 vehicle operations plus vehicle maintenance divided by vehicle revenue hours for the River LINE (Hybrid Rail); the full average including administration is $952. Value of time: USDOT 2016 guidance, $13.60/h local personal travel in 2015 dollars, doubled for waiting.
  • Headline measure: reliable trip-miles delivered = trip-miles run and on time (from the delay simulation for the day's timetable; summer mixes 23% hot days) ÷ trip-miles in today's timetable.
  • Still uncertain: repair times are inferred (10 days in 2024–25, 5 in 2026) because no repair records are public; rescue and blockage times are placeholders. Replace them with NJ TRANSIT hold sheets and failure logs.
